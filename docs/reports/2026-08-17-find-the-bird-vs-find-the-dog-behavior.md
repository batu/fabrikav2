# Find the Bird vs Find the Dog: runtime behavior comparison

Date: 2026-08-17  
Scope: current checked-out source under `games/find_the_bird` and `games/find_the_dog`; static source/config/content/test inspection only.

## Executive summary

Find the Bird is no longer merely Find the Dog with bird art and copy. The two games still share the same Phaser hidden-object shell, storage-key lineage, two reveal modes (classic grayscale-to-color and restoration sprite pickup), completion transaction, shop/ad framework, and core analytics vocabulary. Their package scripts and dependency sets are almost identical apart from game identity and Find the Dog's Android dev dependency ([Bird package](../../games/find_the_bird/package.json#L2), [Dog package](../../games/find_the_dog/package.json#L2)).

The material player-facing divergence is:

1. **Failure pressure:** Dog always spends one of three lives on a wrong tap and can reach the fail/continue overlay. Bird ships with health disabled by default, making wrong taps silent analytics-only events; the old punitive loop can be remotely re-enabled ([Bird default](../../games/find_the_bird/src/config/remoteConfigSchema.ts#L93), [Bird wrong-tap branch](../../games/find_the_bird/src/scenes/GameScene.ts#L2009), [Dog unconditional penalty](../../games/find_the_dog/src/scenes/GameScene.ts#L1965)).
2. **Navigation and search feel:** Bird limits pinch zoom to 1.75x rather than Dog's 2.5x, supports panning at minimum zoom for cover-fitted continuous scenes, and adds inertial pan. Bird also raises square-level minimum hit targets and resolves overlaps by nearest center ([Bird pinch](../../games/find_the_bird/src/scenes/PinchZoom.ts#L21), [Dog pinch](../../games/find_the_dog/src/scenes/PinchZoom.ts#L21), [Bird hit geometry](../../games/find_the_bird/src/scenes/hitboxGeometry.ts#L10)).
3. **Hints and onboarding:** Dog's tutorial consumes no hint on its tutorial hint step, then normal hints choose a random unfound target. Bird fires a real tutorial hint, waits until that hinted bird is collected before teaching zoom, prefers an on-screen target, and draws an edge arrow for off-screen hints ([Dog hint flow](../../games/find_the_dog/src/scenes/GameScene.ts#L3205), [Bird hint flow](../../games/find_the_bird/src/scenes/GameScene.ts#L3811), [Bird edge arrow](../../games/find_the_bird/src/scenes/GameScene.ts#L3897)).
4. **Economy and retention:** Dog caps free hint balance at three and grants achievement rewards automatically on unlock. Bird removes the hint cap, makes achievement rewards explicitly claimable, and adds a daily streak claim ([Dog cap](../../games/find_the_dog/src/core/Constants.ts#L57), [Bird uncapped grant](../../games/find_the_bird/src/core/GameState.ts#L717), [Bird streak claim](../../games/find_the_bird/src/core/GameState.ts#L1142), [Bird achievement claim](../../games/find_the_bird/src/core/GameState.ts#L1361)).
5. **Content/runtime maturity:** Bird has a much larger current catalog and a non-empty bundled manifest; Dog's current catalog lists 54 levels but its bundled manifest explicitly contains zero. That is operationally consequential offline, not merely a count difference ([Bird bundled manifest](../../games/find_the_bird/public/levels/bundled-manifest.json#L1), [Bird catalog](../../games/find_the_bird/public/levels/catalog-manifest.json#L1), [Dog bundled manifest](../../games/find_the_dog/public/levels/bundled-manifest.json#L1), [Dog catalog](../../games/find_the_dog/public/levels/catalog-manifest.json#L1)).

## Intentional differences vs drift/bugs

### Strong evidence of intentional product differences

- **Bird's fail-free default** is explicitly documented as a retention decision in remote config, and its code removes the entire punishment vocabulary when disabled. This is intentional, even though it leaves three-life state and fail offers dormant for remote reactivation.
- **Bird's lower zoom ceiling, continuous cover-fit/pan behavior, forgiving square hitboxes, visible-first hints, edge arrow, claimable achievement rewards, and daily streak claim** all have dedicated modules/tests or comments tied to dated device/review decisions. These are not incidental copy changes.
- **Species copy, product IDs, native identifiers, analytics endpoints, art, and catalogs** are expected reskin boundaries. Internal `dog` naming in Bird is technical debt but does not by itself change behavior.
- **Achievement catalog composition** is intentionally different: Bird replaces Dog's best-time/mastery achievements with longer bird-count, completion, and streak ladders, while keeping persisted IDs for inherited ladders where practical.

### Likely implementation drift worth reconciling

- **Platform hardening exists only in Bird.** Bird releases its WebGL probe context, adds frame-rate governing, storage fallback, asset URL helpers, artifact revision checks, snapshot-safe Phaser helpers, resource-release tests, manifest freshness/retry tests, and expanded runtime texture policy. Those are generic shell concerns, not bird-specific rules. Unless Dog is intentionally frozen, these are upstream-candidate fixes rather than product differentiation.
- **Dog's empty bundled manifest is a concrete release risk.** The checked-in file says `levels: []` while the catalog contains 54 entries. The runtime may still work online or from cache, but no source-only reading can turn zero bundled entries into offline coverage. This should be treated as a release/config defect until a product decision says Dog is online-only.
- **Bird advertises `build:android` without declaring `@capacitor/android`.** Dog declares it. Workspace hoisting can hide this locally, so a clean-install build is required before calling it a bug; it is nevertheless suspicious package drift.
- **Bird's pickup-style selector looks like evaluation tooling exposed in normal Settings.** The module explicitly calls it a session-only preference and lists six experimental styles. If production is meant to have one canonical find animation, this is accidental surface area; if player customization is intended, it needs a durable product decision and likely persistence.
- **Shared storage keys and `dog` domain terms in Bird** preserve migration compatibility, but also create cross-game collision risk if both web apps ever share an origin/storage namespace. Native apps normally isolate storage, so this is a conditional architecture risk, not a demonstrated player bug.

## Tests and configuration inspected

- Static inventory: Bird has **58 unit test files, 1 e2e spec, and 1 performance script**; Dog has **33 unit test files and 1 e2e spec**. Both expose the same `vitest run`, TypeScript, ESLint, Vite, and Playwright-style lanes through their package/config files ([Bird scripts](../../games/find_the_bird/package.json#L6), [Dog scripts](../../games/find_the_dog/package.json#L6)).
- The 25 Bird-only unit files concentrate on precisely the newer behavior: achievement claiming/daily rewards, hitbox geometry, level fit, pan inertia, frame-rate governor, health flag, pickup routing, catalog snapshots/manifests, storage fallback, resource release, and artifact revision contracts.
- Current dirty-working-tree verification was run alongside this inspection. **Dog passed all 33 files / 233 tests. Bird passed 56 files and failed 2 files, with 360 tests passing and 3 failing.** The Bird failures are behaviorally relevant: the bundled manifest test expects the starter prefix but sees 34 bundled levels instead of 5; the native public bundle is 149,183,520 bytes against a 104,857,600-byte cap; and restoration cleanup geometry reports 33 blocked birds. These failures reinforce that Bird's larger content set is not currently a clean release state.
- Manifest counts were computed from the checked-in JSON with `jq`: Bird catalog 133 / bundled 34; Dog catalog 54 / bundled 0. Directory counts are larger (Bird 135, Dog 55) because manifest/support files and non-level folders exist; manifest membership is the behaviorally relevant count.

## Confidence and gaps

**High confidence:** wrong-tap rules, zoom limits, pan inertia, hint selection, hint cap, reward-claim semantics, pickup options, remote defaults, package dependency differences, checked-in manifest counts, and the current test outcomes. The behavioral claims are direct executable-code/config observations; the test totals come from current dirty-working-tree runs.

**Medium confidence:** the claim that Bird's generic hardening should be backported to Dog. The code is clearly generic, but repository history/product ownership was not sufficient to prove whether Dog is maintained, frozen, or deliberately kept on the older shell.

**Not established by this report:**

- Which remote-config values are live in production. A live override can make Bird punitive or alter ads/economy behavior despite source defaults.
- Which manifest/sequence a real installed build receives from CDN, cache, or cohort assignment.
- Whether browser/e2e/native builds pass, or whether the player-perceived motion/hitbox differences match the comments. Unit verification is known: Dog is green; Bird is not. No runtime, browser, or physical-device evidence was captured.
- Store entitlements, ad availability, actual IAP product mappings, and installed binary identity.
- Whether the empty Dog bundled manifest is intentional online-only policy or an incomplete publish step.

The safe product-level conclusion is therefore: **Bird is the more forgiving, navigable, reward-claim-oriented, and runtime-hardened branch; Dog retains the older limited-lives, higher-zoom, random-hint, capped-free-hint loop.** The safe engineering conclusion is narrower: several Bird-only changes are generic shell fixes and should be audited for upstreaming, while the failure/economy/onboarding differences should remain separate until product explicitly chooses convergence.
