What the device found that tests could not

Every one of these passed unit tests and looked fine in the code.

  1. Every text row on the card was one slot too low — the name sat on the ribbon, the copy ran off the card. The manifest's bands were estimates I never checked against the art.
  2. Tier 3 ran off the right edge, losing its deck and third perch: the house was scaled to a cover-fitted background that is far wider than the phone.
  3. Birds stood beside their perch, not on it. A sparrow's tail pulls its sprite centre ~12% left of its legs, so centring put the feet too far right.
  4. Magenta keying residue rimmed the house sprites — a purple outline I did not see and the gate did. Down 95%, silhouette untouched.
  5. The beanie's pom-pom was clipped off by the porthole, losing the detail the hat state is read by.
  6. The empty-perch marker read as a pale smudge, and separately floated above the branch because the markers carried transparent padding the bird sprites do not.

Two more in the harness: HomeScene's shutdown clears the whole HUD overlay, so a late teardown was silently removing the page I had just driven to; and VITE_INSITU_TOUR_STATE was never an allowlisted env key, so every capture build walked the entire tour instead of parking on one state.

Bird tags

All 855 birds across 44 levels now tagged, up from 186, on gemini-3.8-flash via OpenRouter after agy's quota died.

The audit caught something that mattered: wrens, chickadees, finches and thrushes were carrying the sparrow tag, because the broad classifier defaults small brown cartoon birds to "sparrow" — and sparrow is the only tag this release spends. Every sparrow tag now faces a stricter binary check, and anything unconfirmed is demoted. That trades recall for precision on purpose: a false positive makes a wren visibly tick the sparrow card, a false negative only slows the ladder. 150 tags became 78, and agreement went from 27/40 to 40/40.
