# P3 Explorer Bluebird mascot lock

Status: approved canonical mascot for Find the Bird.

## Source sheets

- `02_p3-explorer-bluebird-turnaround.png` — identity, silhouette, views, avatar crop, and palette
- `03_p3-explorer-bluebird-expressions-tools.png` — expressions, movement, and permitted tool interactions

When generated art conflicts with this document, preserve the identity rules below and regenerate the art.

## Identity

- Compact, rounded, upright storybook bluebird
- Cobalt-blue head, back, wings, and tail
- Warm-cream face surround, throat, and belly
- One large friendly visible eye in profile; two symmetrical eyes in front view
- Small, short charcoal beak
- Olive field cap and tied neckerchief
- Brown cross-body satchel resting at the bird's left hip
- Short charcoal legs and simple bird feet
- Confident, curious, kind expression; never manic, infantile, or stern

## Canonical palette

- Cobalt blue: `#1473C4`
- Warm cream: `#F1E4C4`
- Field olive: `#7C852F`
- Leather brown: `#A65E25`
- Charcoal: `#292D2F`
- Reward gold: `#E2A62B`
- Kraft edge: `#B77A3D`

Small value shifts are allowed for layer separation. Hue drift is not.

## Proportions

- Head and torso form one compact egg silhouette.
- Full body is approximately 1.45 times as tall as it is wide.
- Eye diameter is approximately one quarter of the head width.
- Beak projects less than one quarter of the body width.
- Legs occupy approximately one fifth of the standing height.
- Cap brim stays above the eye and never hides more than its upper edge.
- Satchel is approximately one quarter of the torso area.

## Anatomy and tool rules

- The mascot always has exactly two anatomical wings and two legs.
- Wings replace arms. Never add shoulders, human arms, hands, palms, fingers, or separate gripping limbs.
- A small tool may be held between overlapping folded wing feathers.
- Maps and larger objects rest on the ground, hang from a strap, or attach to the satchel.
- Rolled guides, binoculars, and compasses should default to straps or clips.
- Tool use must not change the wing silhouette into a human hand.
- Keep the bird stylized and upright; do not drift toward realistic bird anatomy.

## Cardboard rendering

- Bold, flat-shaded cardstock shapes with crisp die-cut edges
- Thin corrugated-cardboard edge visible only where it clarifies depth
- Large, readable layers with controlled medium-depth shadows
- Restrained paper tooth; surfaces remain calm at phone scale
- No felt, fabric, fuzz, stitching, embroidery, plush, wool, glossy plastic, or photorealism
- Avoid dense surface grain and tiny decorative layers

## Expression set

- Neutral attentive
- Delighted discovery
- Puzzled or thinking
- Focused inspection
- Calm walking
- Celebratory

Expressions come from eye direction, eyelid shape, beak opening, head angle, and wing pose. Do not add eyebrows, lips, teeth, or human facial anatomy.

## Generation anchor

Use both source sheets as identity references. State that the task is an extension of the canonical P3 Explorer Bluebird, not a redesign. Repeat the anatomy, costume, palette, and cardboard-rendering constraints in every production prompt.
